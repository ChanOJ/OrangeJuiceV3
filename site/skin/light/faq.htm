What is Orange Juice ?
Orange Juice is the first demoscene-related portal. Its goal is to be an exhaustive information center about all the activities related to the demoscene. Orange Juice is also called OJ or OJuice. It was first published on the Internet the 5th of April 1997. You're actually watching its third version (called OJv3).

OJ is edited by a french non-profit organisation which is also called Orange Juice. See the contact rubric for more details.
 
What is the demoscene ?
The demoscene is a world wide community of people sharing the same passion in doing demos and related suff. It is mainly composed of males aged between 15 and 25.
 
What is a demo ?
A demo is a way to show others what some demomakers can do in programming (that's the coding part), music (zik) drawing (gfx) and/or modeling (3D). It is a kind of non-interactive multimedia presentation, the difference with a classical animation being that the display of a demo is computed in real time (like people playing a play contrary to showing a movie) making a biggest challenge (because of some computing power considerations).
For now a demo is mostly composed of 3D animations mixed with 2D effects.

There are three main kinds or types of demos :
- intro 4k : limited size and no music (mostly)
- intro 64k : limited size, no other restrictions.
- demo : virtualy no limit.

Restrictions change from a competition to another, it mostly depends of the machine the programs are runned on (486 or Pentium III ?... :) ). The demos are mostly designed to run on PC computers, but not that long away there were mostly designed to run on C64, Atari and Amiga. We can even find demos running on TO7, BeBox, RISC PC, Macintosh, Amstrad CPC, Gameboy, Playstation, etc...
 
Who are the demomakers ?
They're mostly students; quite young people (between 15 and 25) who are computer addicts spending days (well, nights actually) on coding their demos. The always have a pseudonym, their real name being not used at all.

The countries known for demomaking are Scandinavia (Norway, Sweden and Finland), Denmark, Germany, The Netherlands, Switzerland, Poland and France. Some eastern countries (Russia, ...) recently arrived with some pretty good productions. Spain, Italy, Japan and the United States are less active. The other countries are still starting in this domain.

As a demo usually takes quite long to make, (not a day but days, and week, and months...), and as a coder is not always good in composing ziks, the work is divided, demomakers group themselves, thus creating demogroups.
A group is like a firm, it has it's own name and reputation, for example: Future Crew, Impact Studios, Pulse (not OJ, we don't code (yet...)).
They meet themselves on the chats networks (IRC) and in real life during the demoparties.
 
What is a demoparty ?
The parties are regularly organized in order to gather the scene (the demomakers) together for several days non-stop (mostly 3 days and 2 nights). Organized are: competition for demos, intro, modules (or ziks) and gfx. Some others are the floppy disk throwing competition, same for hard drives or even computers (!) etc.

The biggest parties are the Assembly (Finland), The Party (Danemark) and The Gathering (Norway). They gather 2000 to 4300 people !

But the biggest are not the best because the ambiance is altered by the big number of gamers who spend their whole time to play games instead of participating to the demoscene. So the best parties are the small and medium ones. For instance The Takeover (Holland), the Mekka-Symposium (Germany) and the LTP (France) are three great meetings.

There is an average of one party per week in the whole Europe.
 
How to make a demo ?
Actually, a group's coder spends his free(or work) time programming small programs. These routines are the ones who synchronize the music, display pictures, or do the so called "effects". Routines are usually written in assembler (asm), C/C++ or Pascal. In order to make a good looking demo coders will optimize some parts (the ones who use a lot of cpu time) in asm. C and Pascal, which are "clear" languages are used to link asm routines. Finaly, a few days before the demo's presentation (sometimes even a few hours or minutes) routines are reunified, and there it is: ..... the demo....

Graphics are the work of graphicians, and it's often a pixel by pixel work. The graphician role is also to modelize the 3D objects, to place them in a virtual 3D world and to set the path which will be followed by the virtual camera coded by the coder.

The musician makes, creates and composes the musik that can be heard while pictures and special effect are shown on the screen (50 square meters at a party :) ).
It's usually synchronized with the different events of the demo (rythm changes, breaks). This music is made with a tracker, a program for writing music on many sound-tracks, using samples (simply the recording of an instrument or a noise, or whatever). For those who know a bit on computer music, it's like a midi file, except you don't use the sound-card instrument, but your own. The whole makes a module, it can be recognized by it's extention, mod, s3m, it, xm and some others which are less common. Let say that more and more the music format used is the MP3 instead of the modules.

Demomaking is not an easy job, the technical level is very high. The coders have to know how to code fast effects, the graphicians to make beautiful draws and to modelize well-looking 3D objects using the less faces as possible, and the musicians to compose great musics using the less instrument as possible (in the case of modules) to make the demo-size not become too big.

Have a look to the resources page.

Where does the demoscene come from ?
Since the beginning of personal computers (not only PCs) (early 80') there are pirates (now we call them hackers, but it's not the best word). They modify commercial software (that's cracking) to enable their copiing.

To show their talents, they signed (it's less common these days) their work by adding visual and sound effects at the beginning of the program. After some evolution, the scene was born, the main difference is: the scene has nothing more to to do with pirating.
