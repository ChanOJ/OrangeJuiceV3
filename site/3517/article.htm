THE MYSTERY OF PRESELECTION
by netpoet/park

After a couple of conversations I had with people who wanted to talk about
and criticise the way we preselected songs at Mekka & Symposium 2002 I felt
that this might be a good time to write an article about how I personally
like to preselect.

PLEASE take your time to read this or don't read it at all! I really like
to spread information about how this issue works and I took my time to do
so, so please be my guest and learn from it, but

TAKE
YOUR
TIME
!

1st idea: Objective preselection is impossible.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Whoever has done anything like this or even just thought about how a
preselection might look like will have noticed that a completely
objective preselection is impossible. Everybody (and I mean EVERYBODY)
sticks to his or her musical taste up to a certain amount. The objective
component comes into play when you know that things work this way and
that you have to try keeping this aspect as small as possible.
So - of course - it happens that when I listen to a track that I
personally don't like AT ALL I say, oh god, this style SUCKS!
Still, this does NOT mean that I rate it lower because of the style
choice. Rather, I say this because I want to show the other jury members
that my ranking might result of the fact that the style is not actually
my taste.

Please also note that, resulting out of the fact that there is no REALLY
objective preselection, there have always been and will always be
different opinions about song choices.

2nd idea: The allround compo list
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
It is our task as a preselection jury to present a good competition to
the audience. Especially in the MP3 compos, the entries increase a lot.
It happens very often that many songs hit a certain style, and according
to this we cannot play all of them, no matter how good they are as
this would result in a really boring compo that would just go onto
people's nerves. So we try to add a little bit of this and a little bit
of that, but always dependant of the quality of every track, of course.

3rd idea: I can only talk for myself
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
This whole article only represents my own attitude towards preselection.
You might ask somebody else and he might consider me an idiot.
Who knows.
So all you can read here is basically what I think about a just compo
preselection. And by the way, I'd be thankful for constructive criticism
and I'd be glad to explain an aspect of what I'm talking about here
if it's not clear to you.
BUT
leave the usual old "So you think you're perfect?!? You can't even X"
blabla out. Nobody's perfect, and I can only say that I'm trying to
do the best job possible. Whoever has done anything like this knows
that there are always people complaining.

4th idea: Preselection and own entry - you CAN'T DO THAT!
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
In a one-person-jury I would agree to you. At Mekka, we always have at
least 3 people in the jury, and whenever I have a track among the compo
entries I let the others decide. The exact ranking system I will explain
later but when I have an own track in the compo, my given points will be
the average of the other two opinions.

5th idea: But you push your own group's entries at least!
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
If you knew about the arguments I've had with friends of mine about why
I didn't play their tracks you wouldn't say that.

6th idea: What a compo is all about
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Compo" comes from "competition", and as we deal with music compos it
must be clear that we have a struggle of musicians for the best possible
place. This is done by showing everyone's abilities to make sound.
That means, of course, that when you enter a really good sounding track
whose only problem is that it bases on only one pattern that you show
that you can make good overall sound but didn't manage (or care) to
make a whole _song_ out of the pattern. I see this as one pattern
(maybe with a bit of song structure) that sounds good. When somebody
else enters a track with loads of good song ideas and who might even
have a slightly worse overall sound, then I rank this track higher.
It's about making songs after all, not patterns. The first song might
catch a really good place in a jingle compo though.

7th idea: The ranking system
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Usually - depending on the number of entries - we listen to every track
and give points after that. The number of points results out of several
aspects that I will describe in the hints section.
Having heard all entries, we compare the sum of the points we gave and
have a look at how many tracks we want to play. Depending on the styles
in the best tracks, we play a certain number of tracks. Usually this
number varies from 10 to 14, sometimes we play even more if we think
that maybe one track might be a funny insert that will give the audience
time to breathe between two heavy tracks.

8th idea: What "preselection" means
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
There are two big opinions around about what "pre-selecting" actually
means. According to my job of trying to set up a good compo for the
audience, I say that it means that a jury has to "pre-select" certain
tracks for the compo.
Some people think it means that the thrown out tracks are preselected.
Not that it REALLY changed anything about the work I do in this section,
but I don't like the idea of kicking tracks out instead of taking them
in.

So you've never or hardly ever been preselected and want to change it?
It's not as hard as you think. The following hints might give you an
idea what mistakes are usually made.

--- THE GOOD OLD HINTS SECTION --------------------------------------------

1.) Don't track at parties!
I've never ever EVER understood how people dare tracking at a party.
All I can tell you is that I could never really do that, even with
my Beyerdynamic headphones. They're closed and in professional
studios, they're standard equipment. If you don't have closed head-
phones you will hardly hear ANYTHING in the great noise. But even
WITH those headphones you will never hear the fine things in the
track. Sometimes we get entries that show clearly that volumes don't
match at all, or the bass is too loud. Things like that just happen.
You can't change that. MAYBE you might find half an hour of silence
at the party that allows you work on your track, but in your place
I wouldn't bet on it.

Come with a done track! Ready to go!

2.) Don't mix and master with headphones!
This is something I learned from Virgill/Haujobb first (thanks!).
If you want to know more basic things about how to mix and master
or how to record vocals or anything similar, there are a lot of
tutorials out there. If you like, check this page:

http://getimo.de/linkpage2/html/tuts_common.php

If you don't want to read all this, just note that your ears can
easily get tired, too! Just relax, take a break and listen to
the track later with good speakers, and you'll see that you
ruined it by making the bass and treble too loud or similar
stuff you will regret.

3.) Don't use bad samples!
Especially in MP3s, but nowadays even in tracked formats, the quality
of the sound is very important. That means for example that an
unwanted background noise shows that you were unable to reduce the
noise or didn't care about it.
Due to the bad sound and maybe the few possibilities that old tracker
programs offer, it has never happened in the preselections that I was
part of that we played ANY .dbm-file. Either this format offers only
so bad sound possibilities that you CAN'T make acceptable sound or
the composers who track in it are content with the fact that they're
old school.
Especially in MP3 you DO have the possibilities to get rid of
unwanted sounds. Use them!

4.) Make something happen in the track!
I mentioned it before, but this is the reason why I had to give a
couple of tracks that had a really good overall sound a relatively
small number of points. Give your whole song structure! Show me
that you know about making it interesting! It's not like I want to
kick songs out, I rather want to PLAY them. By not adding a quiet
part in the middle with a good idea in it you might have just
wasted your chance to have your track played.

5.) Don't rip!
Don't try looking good with other people's fame. As soon as I find
out that it's not your work, this will cost you more points than
you can afford usually.

6.) Don't come with the 1000th version of the 2nd reality soundtrack!
This is basically the same as the "don't rip!"-hint, but it also
covers the general idea that even making cover versions of known (and
maybe good) tracks doesn't necessarily get you further. Trying to
"play with the big ones" might look bad when you're not at all their
class. It always depends on the special case, of course, but just
ripping the patterns of the 2nd reality soundtrack (and this HAS
happened) and adding some beats does NOT get you into the compo only
because the original song was good.

7.) Come to the point! (1) (song structure)
Have a look at your song. Is it really structured? How long does it
take to come to the main part? Is there a main part at all? All these
things we care about. If you have a great main part that shows from
3:00 to 3:15, there might be something weird about it, don't you think?
Maybe the intro was WAY too long, maybe you just played a couple of
unnecessary patterns before. Just try keeping the song structured in
a way that entertains the audience. It's my job as preselector to have
a close look at this aspect, too.

8.) Come to the point! (2) (time limit)
All music compos I know have a time limit. This has a reason. The
reason is that - again - we want to present a good competition.
We want to entertain the audience, not to go onto their nerves.
The second thought is that - if you're a good musician - you know how
to present your knowledge and your skills. The time limit was set by
people who know how long it takes to present your skills. Believe me,
it IS possible to do so. Sometimes I'm told that 3 minutes were WAY
too little to present good drum n bass or techno or trance or whatever.
You're wrong.
And if you desperately HAVE to make a special version of the track, then
do it. Personally, I don't think it's a good idea to cut tracks for a
compo. The reason for this is that normally you totally ruin the song
structure by doing so. What we listen to afterwards usually sounds like
an unfinished track, which will cost you points, I can assure you.
Generally produce tracks for a compo! If you know that your time limit
is 3 minutes then you will produce for it and not cut an 8 minute track
down to 3 minutes.

9.) Don't use loops only!
This is more a part of "don't rip!", but especially in tracked music
compos, we DO look at the patterns to see what's happening and if the
chords and beats you use are tracked or just inserted loops. Don't
think we don't have a chance of noticing this in MP3s, but in tracked
compos it's even easier, so watch out for this.

10.) "This track sucks."
Sometimes this aspect makes my job really easy, but I just want to
mention this aspect so that even the dumbnuts on earth get a chance to
understand. Maybe it's not the best idea of all to write into your
song info that you think the track is bad, unfinished, that it sucks
or whatever. It's like excusing for something before having said it.
I personally have a very strong attitude to just kick the track out
only because of this note. If you think you're track isn't good, then
GO HOME AND MAKE A GOOD ONE and come back afterwards.
This is a compo and not a pool for unfinished shit you just want
ANYbody to give a listen to.

11.) Keep the rules!
We don't set up rules for nothing. When I see a track whose size is
over the allowed one, I have no problem to take it out, even if it's
only 1 byte we're talking about. I do this because of the fairness
concerning everybody else. They produce for the compo and keep the
rules, and usually there are so many other good tracks that we don't
die because of your track not being among them. You might think that
I'm a pedant because of that, but if you were in my position and had
more than enough well playable tracks, you would do the same thing.
It's only fair to disqualify the tracks first that don't match the
rules. The other break of rules we usually have is a too long track.
Usually at compos there are rules what will happen to the track if
this rule is broken (fadeout etc). This MIGHT result in this track
being played and just faded out at the end, but if it concerns the
song structure (as mentioned above), it might also result in the track
not being preselected.

12.) General stuff
a) Keep the formats!
This is more a personal thing for the Mekka preselection, but:
When you enter an mp3 entry, then we'd be thankful if you stuck
to mp3 or ogg. You MAY enter the tracks as audio-cd, but doing so,
you ruin your chance to choose the fitting bitrate and name for
your own track as we'll have to do that.
And of course, we won't at all listen to your track if you hand in
a format that we've never heard of and that isn't allowed by the
rules.

b) All entries on separate media!
Also please enter ALL ENTRIES ON SEPARATE MEDIA! It happens way too
often that one group enters all entries on one cd, and at first it
sounds like a good idea. But you should keep in mind that all compos
have different preselection teams, and if the cd with all entries
is at one desk, the other group might not take concern of your track
because it's at the wrong desk. Blanks are not THAT expensive anyway.

c) If unusual format: include the programs needed!
The rules usually describe very clearly what formats are allowed.
If you produced on a format that the rules allow and that's still
not usual (like .xm, .it, .mt2, .mp3, .ogg etc.), it might be a good
idea to include the fitting player and maybe even a wave exporter.

d) Don't try discussing preselection issues with me during the compo!
When the compo starts, I - of course - know all tracks. Still I like
to sit in front and give a listen to the whole compo to see if the
tracks really sound the way we expected them to. The problem is that
sometimes the actual stereo system sound varies BIG time from the
sound that we get when we listen to all tracks with our headphones,
so this is an interesting fact for me. I also like to see if the
audience is really entertained or if we made a mistake in taking the
one of the other track. Life's school anyway.
But the least thing I want to do is talk to you about the
preselection during or shortly after the compo. During the compo,
my job is still running, and shortly after the compo emotions are
usually still boiling. I know this very well from the adrenaline
I feel when one of my tracks is played. So if you want to discuss
things with me, I try to be there for you, but please wait until
everything's calmed down. This will bring both of us way better
results.
Something else I usually don't do is talking about whose track made
it or didn't make it into the compo. Please don't ask me if your
track is in or out. This job is hard for me, too, and I don't like
to hurt anyone. Just wait and you'll see...these are the rules
and you agreed to play by them. And also maybe you don't want to
know the answer either.

Thanks for your time,

/netpoet of park (frank stiegler)
